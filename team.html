Leadership

Who Runs GoWildAtlas

Dr. James Carter

Founder & Programme Director

James is a wildlife veterinarian with more than 14 years of field experience across East and Southern Africa. He founded GoWildAtlas to give veterinary students and professionals structured access to the kind of practical wildlife medicine that cannot be learned from a lecture hall.

His work spans large mammal immobilisation, health screening, translocation support, and conservation medicine for lion, elephant, rhino, buffalo, and wild dog populations.

BVSc Veterinary Medicine MSc Wildlife Health RCVS Registered

Emma Whitfield

Head of Operations & Participant Experience

Emma oversees the participant journey from first enquiry to final field debrief. She coordinates applications, travel guidance, accommodation, safety checks, and the small details that make a demanding programme feel calm and well-run.

Her background in conservation operations and student support keeps each placement organised, responsive, and grounded in participant welfare.

BSc Conservation Biology 10+ years field operations
In the Field

Our Wildlife Vet Team

Every programme is supported by a lead field vet or conservation clinician who understands the local species, field conditions, and welfare responsibilities of that ecosystem.

South Africa

Dr. Peter Carter

Peter is a Big Five field veterinarian with deep experience in immobilisation, herd health, and reserve-based conservation medicine. He is known for calm teaching in high-pressure field situations.

Namibia

Dr. Olivia Bennett

Olivia focuses on desert-adapted wildlife and community conservancy work. Her field interests include remote anaesthesia, post-release monitoring, and practical decision-making in arid environments.

Botswana

Dr. Daniel Brooks

Daniel works across wetland and savannah systems, with a focus on large mammal health, aerial support, and conservation interventions in difficult terrain.

Tanzania & Zanzibar

Dr. Rebecca Moore

Rebecca brings a broad wildlife medicine background across savannah and coastal ecosystems, connecting clinical fieldwork with disease surveillance and conservation education.

Costa Rica

Dr. Hannah Reed

Hannah specialises in tropical rescue and rehabilitation, with a focus on triage, wound care, release planning, and low-stress handling for rainforest species.

Ecuador

Dr. Claire Morgan

Claire works at the intersection of marine conservation, rainforest medicine, and endemic species care, helping students connect clinical skills with ecosystem-level thinking.
